# Field campaign of 21 November 2026

First campaign. Thirty testers, 341 findings, 97 distinct problems, 32 issues (#149 to #180), one crash fixed the
same day.

## Setup

- **Product under test:** `main` after #182 (commit `3ed617b`), without two small fixes merged later that day.
  Version 0.16.1 from source.
- **Hosts:** every tester was a subagent of one Claude Code session (version 2.1.328, macOS), without subagents of
  its own, so each reviewed its boards one after the other. Testers and reproductions ran on a Sonnet model; the
  grouping of findings ran on an Opus model. Codex was not used.
- **Tools:** mocks only. No account, no key, no network.
- **Testers:** the thirty of [`campaign.json`](../campaign.json): 18 personas, 3 scenarios played by 3 teams each,
  3 teammates on one repository.

## Same scenario, three teams

The scripted part of a scenario is deterministic, so the threshold is zero: a single key that differs between two
teams is a defect and gets an issue.

| Scenario | Keys compared | Result |
|---|---|---|
| s1, a feature on personal data | 13 | Identical for the three teams |
| s2, a decision with a market dossier | 14 | Identical for the three teams |
| s3, a bug with a dependency alert | 14 | One key differed (`consultation`) for one team |

The s3 difference was not a defect: that team imported the dependency alert before the consultation instead of
after, so its consultation carried the alert. Played again in the scripted order, the three fingerprints are
identical. `field.py cohorts` now plays the scripted part without an agent, and the test suite runs it.

In the free part, the teams of a scenario reached the same routes and the same packs, and differed where a person
would: which evidence a board cited, and how many of the result's answers they filled before giving up.

## What the campaign found

The lesson is weight. The product asked too much for a small change: too many rules in a profile, too many answers
to close a task, too much text for an agent to read.

| Problem | Testers | Issue |
|---|---|---|
| `task route` and `task consult` crashed on the component-scoped patterns that `init` writes | 15 | fixed the same day |
| Closing a small task asked for 50 to 180 answers, one per rule of the profile | 14 | #149, #150, #151 |
| Consultation, board pass and handoff ran from 75 to 220 KB, the scope repeated for each board | 14 | #157 |
| Deliberation sent each objection to every board | 13 | #158 |
| Setup preselected dozens of methods foreign to the project | 11 | #152 |
| The guard said "Edit refused" in `observe` mode while it let the edit through | 10 | #169 |
| `init` proposed no project check | 10 | #168 |
| Tool mocks told the story of another project | 9 | #162 |
| A change of rules silently invalidated approved tasks; a forged record passed `task list` and `check` | 15 | #154 |
| `--set` accepted credential-shaped values and wrote them to committed files | 7 | #155 |
| `task complete` said `done` with sensitive rules and disagreements left open | 7 | #151 |
| The mandatory GDPR evidence pushed the agent to write the data controller's documents | 6 | #160 |
| A teammate's tasks were invisible | 6 | #161 |
| Routing read "no payments" as "payment" | 5 | #156 |

The other issues, #153, #159, #163 to #167, #170 to #180, cover the remaining groups; none was left without one.

## What worked well

Counted over the 24 written reports:

1. The person's decision: the count of verdicts, the warning when approving against the boards' advice, the typed
   task ID (about 17 reports).
2. Refusals that give the next step: invalid or duplicate IDs, an empty brief, malformed JSON, `init` run twice
   (about 15).
3. Strict validation of board entries: evidence required for an observed finding, positions, evidence references
   (about 15).
4. Tool mocks that let a team rehearse offline and without an account (about 14).
5. `host apply` and `host check`, honest about what is applied and what is left to the person (about 12).
6. The guard in `enforce`: a refusal with its reason, control files protected under several spellings of a path, the
   covering task named (about 9).
7. An honest closing: `task complete` refuses an unmet criterion or a failed command (about 8).
8. A tampered record caught by `task export`, `handoff` and `guard --ci` (about 7).
9. A scriptable setup with `init --questions` and `--answers` (about 7).
10. Deliberation and `task challenge`, which make a disagreement visible at the moment of deciding (about 7).

## Limits

- The testers are agents playing people: they find what a careful newcomer finds, not what a team feels after a
  month. No real team took part.
- One host and one model family played every tester. Subagent reviews, Codex, and a host other than these two were
  not exercised by this campaign (see [`../../host-validation/reports/`](../../host-validation/reports/)).
- Every tool was a mock. Nothing was verified against a real server.
- The testers' projects, full reports and reproduction projects are not in the repository.
